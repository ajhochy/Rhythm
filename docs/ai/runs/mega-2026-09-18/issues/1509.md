# #1509 Electron: improve reading comfort in Tasks and Agents chat [OPEN] labels=enhancement,frontend

## Problem

Rhythm Electron's dark Tasks list is harder to read comfortably than the desktop sidebar reference AJ supplied on September 17. The difference combines very bright text on a nearly black green background, small relatively heavy titles, tiny metadata, and repeated visual separators/status labels.

AJ requested this issue to capture the proposed improvements. Evaluate the shared color and typography treatment on Tasks, then apply the agreed reading-comfort improvements to Agents chat as required scope. Compare like-for-like content at the same zoom on both surfaces.

## Evidence

- Task titles are declared at **12px, weight 570, line-height 1.35**; metadata is **9px**. The title/metadata gap is **1px**, within a **56px** row.
- Every row has a bottom border, a completion circle, and a secondary status/date line. In the supplied Past Due group, `Open · Past due` repeats under every title although the heading already communicates the date category.
- Approximate screenshot samples: reference text `#DEDFDF` on `#252727` (~11.3:1); Rhythm text `#EBF4F0` on `#060F0E` (~17.3:1). These are image samples, not extracted reference-app design tokens or a formal accessibility audit. Higher contrast is not inherently worse; the goal is AJ's preferred reading comfort while preserving accessibility.
- The reference appears to use larger regular-weight titles and fewer interruptions. Its exact font/weight was not verified. Rhythm declares `Inter, system-ui, sans-serif`; the actual rendered face must be checked before attributing the difference to a particular font.

Source: [Tasks styles](https://github.com/ajhochy/Rhythm/blob/main/apps/web/src/pages/tasks/styles.css), [shared theme](https://github.com/ajhochy/Rhythm/blob/main/apps/web/src/styles.css).

## Proposed changes

1. **Improve the words first:** try 14px task titles, weight 400–450, with a 20px line-height. Keep the current font family initially to isolate the effect of size and weight.
2. **Soften the dark palette:** start with a charcoal background around `#252727`, primary text around `#DEDFDF`, and secondary text around `#A3AAA8`. A subtle green tint can remain. Preserve teal for selection, keyboard focus, and meaningful status.
3. **Make metadata readable and useful:** use 11–12px secondary text; omit redundant `Open · Past due` when the group already communicates it. Preserve meaningful distinctions such as read-only/source information, priority, specific dates, and non-default statuses.
4. **Reduce segmentation:** remove or substantially soften per-row rules, retaining useful group separators and clear hover/selection states. Preserve visible completion controls, usable hit areas, and keyboard focus.

The proposed primary and secondary colors on `#252727` are approximately 11.3:1 and 6.4:1. Treat the values as starting points for visual comparison, not a mandate to lower contrast everywhere. Avoid solving this by fading all text or merely adding more empty row space.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At normal zoom, task titles use an agreed readable size/weight near the proposed 14px / 400–450 / 20px settings; meaningful metadata is at least 11px.
- [ ] A same-content, same-window-size, same-zoom before/after comparison demonstrates the proposed calmer dark treatment; AJ accepts the reading comfort before app-wide rollout.
- [ ] Redundant status/date text is omitted where the grouping already communicates it, while meaningful exceptions and accessible task context remain available.
- [ ] Hover, selected, completed, disabled, and keyboard-focus states remain distinguishable. Completion and row-action hit areas remain at least 44×44 CSS px.
- [ ] Normal text meets WCAG AA's 4.5:1 minimum across actual relevant backgrounds, including hover and selection. Controls and focus indicators retain applicable accessibility contrast.
- [ ] Long titles, narrow layouts, 200% zoom, and light mode retain usable layout, accessible names, and existing task interactions.
