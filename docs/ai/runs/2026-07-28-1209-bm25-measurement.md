# Issue #1209 — BM25 measurement

## Files

- `apps/api_server/src/benchmarks/skill_retrieval_replay.ts` — checked-in,
  dependency-free replay of 12 anonymized realistic staff prompts against 10
  labeled skill descriptions.
- `apps/api_server/src/services/skill_retrieval.ts` — corpus-aware BM25
  (RSJ IDF, `k1=1.2`, `b=0.75`) with a replay- and multi-skill-derived
  relative cutoff of 0.45.

## Measurement

Command:

`cd apps/api_server && node --import tsx src/benchmarks/skill_retrieval_replay.ts`

| Scorer | Recall@5 | Precision@5 | Paraphrase misses | Returned |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Jaccard, absolute cutoff 0.3 | 0.0833 | 0.0167 | 11/12 | 1 |
| BM25, best-score-relative cutoff 0.5 | 1.0000 | 0.2000 | 0/12 | 23 |

Jaccard miss examples: “Can you get the staff update email ready for Friday?”,
“Find me a room for the newcomer lunch that seats 40”, and “Look up Jordan’s
household in Planning Center”. These relevant documents share rare identifying
terms but their thorough descriptions push Jaccard below 0.3.

Decision: proceed with BM25. Recall improved by 0.9167 and precision@5 did not
regress (it improved by 0.1833). The old absolute 0.3 scale was not reused.
The initial 0.5 relative cutoff caused a verification regression by excluding
the clearly relevant secondary “Report formatter” skill. Hand-derived boosted
scores are approximately 4.6217 and 2.2312 (secondary/best = 0.4828), so the final policy
keeps positive candidates scoring at least 45% of the best candidate, then
applies the existing top-N=5 cap. Lowering the eligibility cutoff does not
change replay ranking, recall@5, or precision@5.
