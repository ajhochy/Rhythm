Rhythm — Staff Guide

A friendly walkthrough — how Rhythm turns your recurring work, annual projects, and one-off tasks into a real, doable week. Visalia Christian Reformed Church.

Overview

How Rhythm fits into your week

  1. Capture your intent — one-off tasks, recurring rhythms, and annual projects.
  2. Rhythm generates the work — it turns those into real, dated tasks ahead of each planning window.
  3. Plan your week on the Weekly Planner — review what's due, drag things around, see your calendar shadowed in.
  4. Reserve rooms and message colleagues without leaving the app.
  5. Wire in Planning Center / Google so external context flows in as planning signals.

Just want to start? Skip to Your first 30 minutes for a concrete, no-AI setup you can finish in one sitting.

Mental model

Tasks, Rhythms, Projects, Automations 2-min read

Four words you'll see all over Rhythm. They're close cousins, but each plays a different role — knowing which is which up front saves a lot of "wait, which one do I need?" later.

Task

A single thing you do, once — "Order new envelopes." It has a title, a due date, maybe a project link. Tasks are the lowest-level unit of work; everything else eventually produces one.

Rhythm

A recurring template that generates tasks on a cadence — weekly bulletin proofing, monthly counts, annual budget review. Define it once; the tasks appear on every cycle automatically.

Project

A date-anchored set of related tasks — a Christmas service, a retreat, an annual report. You set a target date and add steps with offsets ("4 weeks before," "1 week after"); Rhythm calendarizes the whole thing.

Automation

A rule that creates work in response to a signal — "when PCO publishes a plan, create a 'confirm volunteers' task." Same tasks as the others, just triggered by something outside Rhythm.

All roads lead to tasksRhythms, Projects, and Automations are different ways of generating tasks. Tasks are what the Weekly Planner shows you. Remember that and you'll never get stuck on "which one do I need?"

Your first 30 minutes Everyone · do this once

Don't want to wire up Claude yet? That's fine — here's the hand-built path. Four steps, about half an hour, and you'll go from an empty app to a Weekly Planner that's actually doing something for you. An empty planner is the fastest way to bounce off Rhythm, so the goal here is simply to get real work flowing into it. Do these in order.

Prefer to let Claude do it?If you'd rather not type any of this in by hand, skip to Let Claude set up Rhythm for you — same outcome, driven by an interview instead. This section is the manual equivalent for staff who aren't taking the Claude path.

Step 1 · Capture three weekly things ~15 min

Think of three things you do every week without fail — proof the bulletin, run rehearsal, reconcile the offering. Each becomes a rhythm on a Weekly cadence so it lands on your planner automatically from now on.

  1. Open Rhythms and click + New Rhythm.
  2. Name it for the recurring work — e.g. "Proof Sunday bulletin." Pick the Weekly cadence.
  3. Add the steps that happen each week (even one step is fine to start). Save.
  4. Repeat twice more so you have three weekly rhythms. Don't aim for complete — aim for three real ones.

Step 2 · Set up one annual/recurring project ~10 min

Pick a single date-anchored event you run each year — a Christmas service, the annual budget, a summer retreat. Build it once as a project template with a handful of steps measured as offsets from the target date; Rhythm calendarizes the whole thing whenever you start an instance.

  1. Open Projects and click + New Template. Give it the event's name and mark it annual if it repeats yearly.
  2. Add 3–5 steps, each with an offset from the target date — e.g. "Pick a theme — 8 weeks before," "Confirm volunteers — 3 weeks before," "Debrief — 1 week after."
  3. Click New Instance, give it this year's target date, and watch Rhythm lay every step onto its correct day.

Step 3 · Look at the Weekly Planner ~2 min

This is the payoff. Open the planner and confirm your new work actually shows up where you'd expect — that's how you know Rhythm is wired correctly.

  1. Open Weekly Planner. Your three rhythms' instances should appear on their cadence days this week.
  2. Navigate to the week your project's first step falls in — the offset step should be sitting on the right day, tagged to the project.
  3. If something isn't there, it almost always means the rhythm or project hasn't been set up yet, or its dates land in a different week — go back to Step 1 or 2 and check the cadence / target date.

Step 4 · Add one external connection ~5 min

Finally, connect one outside system so your week reflects what's already on your calendars. Pick whichever you live in — Planning Center or Google Calendar. Once connected, its events appear as read-only shadow events in the planner so you're planning around reality, not a blank slate.

  1. Open Integrations.
  2. Click Connect next to Planning Center or Google Calendar and sign in with your church account. Approve the read-only scopes.
  3. Wait for the first sync window, then reopen the Weekly Planner — your external events now show up shadowed alongside your tasks.
That's it — you're runningThree weekly rhythms, one annual project, and one calendar feeding your planner. From here, add more as you notice them and explore the rest of this guide. The deeper a foundation you build, the more the Weekly Planner does for you.

Optional · biggest time-saver

Let Claude set up Rhythm for you Recommended · ~20 min

The single biggest win Rhythm offers over a normal task app: you don't have to set up your rhythms, projects, and recurring obligations by hand. Wire Rhythm to your Claude account (Claude Desktop or Claude Code), paste a single interview prompt, and Claude builds the whole skeleton with you — asking the right questions and creating real rhythms, projects, and tasks as you answer.

What you get out of 20 minutesA populated Rhythms list (your real weekly obligations), project templates for your annual events with realistic offsets, and a starter set of tasks. You'll still tweak — but you're tweaking a real foundation instead of staring at an empty screen.
  1. One paragraph on what MCP is. MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the open standard that lets Claude talk to other apps. The Rhythm MCP server exposes Rhythm's tasks, rhythms, projects, and automations to Claude as tools (rhythm_create_rhythm, rhythm_create_project_template, and friends). Once wired up, Claude can create, read, update, and list the same data the desktop app does.
  2. Get your Rhythm API token. In the Rhythm desktop app, open Settings → Claude Integration and copy your session token. (If that pane isn't on your build yet, ask AJ or finance — there's also a CLI fallback documented in the Rhythm MCP server README.)
  3. Add the Rhythm MCP server to Claude. In Claude Desktop, edit ~/Library/Application Support/Claude/claude_desktop_config.json and paste: 
    {
  "mcpServers": {
    "rhythm": {
      "command": "npx",
      "args": ["-y", "@ajhochy/rhythm-mcp-server"],
      "env": {
        "RHYTHM_API_URL": "https://api.vcrcapps.com",
        "RHYTHM_API_TOKEN": "<paste-your-token-here>"
      }
    }
  }
}
    Save and quit + reopen Claude Desktop. (The same JSON drops into Claude Code at ~/.claude/settings.json if that's how you use Claude.)
  4. Confirm it's connected. Open a fresh Claude conversation and type "Call rhythm_ping to check the connection." Claude should respond with the Rhythm API status and the email address your token authenticates as. A 401 means the token's wrong — re-copy it from Settings.
  5. Paste the interview prompt (below) into Claude. Answer its questions — they take 10–20 minutes total. Claude will create your rhythms, projects, and starter tasks directly in Rhythm as you go. Switch to the desktop app to watch them appear.
  6. Spot-check and tweak. When the interview wraps, ask Claude to "show me what you created" — it'll list every rhythm, project, and task with the cadences, target dates, and step offsets. Adjust anything that doesn't fit. Then open the desktop app and start using your week.
The interview prompt — copy this verbatim into ClaudeThis is the load-bearing piece. The same prompt lives in mcp-setup-prompt.md with design notes and variations.
I'd like you to interview me about my role so you can set up Rhythm — a desktop
productivity app for church staff — to match my actual work.

You have access to Rhythm via the rhythm_* MCP tools (rhythm_ping, rhythm_create_rhythm,
rhythm_create_project_template, rhythm_add_project_step, rhythm_create_task,
rhythm_list_rhythms, rhythm_get_dashboard, etc.). Use them as you go.

Please conduct the interview in this order. Ask ONE focused question at a time,
wait for my answer, and only move on when you have what you need:

1. ROLE AND CONTEXT
   - What's my role at the church? (worship leader, pastoral staff, finance/admin,
     facilities, AV/tech, children's ministry, etc.)
   - Roughly how big is the staff and the congregation?
   - What tools do I already use heavily (Planning Center, Google Calendar, a CCM,
     accounting software, etc.)?

2. WEEKLY OBLIGATIONS — the recurring stuff
   - Walk me through a typical week. What do I do every week, without exception?
   - For each recurring thing: what's the cadence (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly)?
     What are the steps inside it?
   - Create one rhythm per recurring obligation with rhythm_create_rhythm; then
     add each step with rhythm_add_rhythm_step.

3. ANNUAL / PROJECT-LIKE WORK — the date-anchored stuff
   - What annual or one-time events do I run? (Christmas Eve, Easter, summer
     retreat, annual budget, year-end report, etc.)
   - For each, what's the target date and what are the milestones, with offsets
     from that date? ("Pick a theme — 8 weeks before," "Send invitations — 4
     weeks before," "Debrief — 1 week after.")
   - Create one project template per recurring event with
     rhythm_create_project_template, then add offset steps with
     rhythm_add_project_step. If it's annual, say so in the description.

4. ONE-OFF TASKS ON MY MIND RIGHT NOW
   - Anything coming up in the next 2-4 weeks that doesn't fit a rhythm or
     project but I shouldn't forget?
   - Create each with rhythm_create_task; set a sensible due date.

5. PEOPLE AND COORDINATION
   - Who do I coordinate with regularly? (Worship leader, pastor, treasurer,
     custodian, etc.)
   - Are any of my rhythms or projects shared with them? Note this in the
     rhythm/project description so I can add them as collaborators later.

GUIDELINES:
- Keep questions short. Don't lecture me about productivity.
- Ask follow-ups when I'm vague ("when you say 'sermon prep,' what are the steps?").
- After each completed item, briefly summarize what you created and move on.
- Don't try to create every possible task — focus on the load-bearing recurring
  work and the major annual events. We can add more later.
- At the end, call rhythm_get_dashboard and show me the summary so I can spot-check.

Start with question 1. Just one question at a time.

Where you land

Dashboard Everyone

The Dashboard is your at-a-glance status. It surfaces what's due soon, the rhythms running right now, and the projects in flight — so you can see the shape of the week in a few seconds.

Quick-add anywhereThe + New Task button is always within reach. Use it when you remember something on the fly — pick a date and it'll appear in the Weekly Planner.
Where you plan

Weekly Planner Everyone

The Weekly Planner is the main pane you'll live in. It lays out the current week day-by-day, grouped by priority, with every generated task, recurring instance, project step, and calendar shadow in one place.

  1. Open Weekly Planner on Monday morning to see everything Rhythm has lined up for the week.
  2. Click a task to open its detail pane on the right — links, due date, notes, related project.
  3. Drag a task to another day if the week shifts; the planner re-flows around it.
  4. Check things off as you go. Anything you don't finish rolls forward — it doesn't quietly disappear.
What shows up in your weekOne-off tasks + active rhythm instances + project-template tasks coming due + calendar shadow events from any connected accounts. If something should be here and isn't, it usually means a rhythm or project hasn't been set up yet (see below).
One-off work

Tasks Everyone

Tasks are the simplest building block — one-off things you need to do. Capture them as soon as they hit your head; assign a due date or week bucket, and they show up in the Weekly Planner automatically.

  1. Click + New Task (top of the list, or from the Dashboard).
  2. Give it a clear, action-oriented title — “Order new envelopes,” not “Envelopes.”
  3. Pick a due date (or week) and, optionally, a project to link it to.
  4. Save. It'll appear on its due day in the Weekly Planner.
Open a task to do moreClicking a task opens the task inspector — where you set notes, link a project, change status, and add collaborators (including an AI collaborator). See Task inspector & collaborators for the full walkthrough.
Editing, archiving, and finding old tasks
  • Click any task in the list to edit the title, date, or notes — changes save automatically.
  • Check off a task to mark it done; finished tasks fall out of the planner but stay searchable.
  • The filter at the top of the Tasks list narrows by status, project, or due window.
Working a task

Task inspector & collaborators Everyone

A task is more than a checkbox. Click any task — in the Tasks list or the Weekly Planner — and the inspector opens in a pane on the right, with the list still in view. This is where you add the detail that makes a task useful: notes, a project link, a status, and collaborators — including Rhythm's standout feature, the AI collaborator.

Opening the inspector & the fields inside

  1. Click a task anywhere it appears — the Tasks list, the Weekly Planner, a project's step list. The inspector slides in on the right; the list behind it stays scrolled where you left it.
  2. Edit the title — changes save as you type, no Save button to hunt for.
  3. Set the due date (or week bucket). This is what positions the task in the Weekly Planner.
  4. Add a project link to file the task under a project — it then shows up tagged to that project in the planner.
  5. Write notes — context, links, a checklist, whatever the future-you will need. Notes are searchable.
  6. Set the status (e.g. open / in progress / done). Marking it done drops it out of the planner but keeps it searchable.
  7. Press Esc or click outside the pane to close the inspector and return to the list.

Collaborators — including the AI collaborator

A task can have collaborators — other people who can see and work the task with you. Rhythm adds a twist most task apps don't: one of those collaborators can be an AI agent. This is how you hand a task off to Claude to research, draft, or move forward while you're elsewhere.

  1. In the inspector, find the Collaborators row and click + Add.
  2. Pick a person (a fellow staff member) to share the task, or pick the AI collaborator — listed as "Visalia CRC" — to bring an agent onto the task.
  3. What happens when you add the AI collaborator: the task gets an attached conversation thread, and the agent can act on the task — reading its notes, asking you clarifying questions, and posting progress back into the thread. You stay in control: it works with you on that specific task rather than running off on its own.
  4. Removing a collaborator: open the same Collaborators row, click the collaborator, and choose Remove. Removing the AI collaborator stops the agent's involvement; the task and its history stay intact.
Why "Visalia CRC" is the AI collaboratorAdding the church's AI collaborator ("Visalia CRC") to a task is the lightweight, per-task way to get Claude's help — distinct from the full MCP onboarding (which sets up your whole account) and from the in-app Agents tab (a deeper, still-in-development integration). Use it when you want a hand on one task.
Long-press & right-click shortcuts
  • Right-click (or Control-click) a task in any list to open a context menu — quick actions like complete, reschedule, link to a project, or delete without opening the full inspector.
  • Long-press a task to pick it up for drag-and-drop — drop it on another day in the Weekly Planner to reschedule, and the planner re-flows around it.
  • These shortcuts do the same things the inspector does — they're just faster when you already know the change you want to make.
Recurring work

Rhythms Everyone

A rhythm is a recurring task template — work that comes around on a schedule. Define it once and Rhythm generates the concrete task instances on every cycle: weekly bulletins, monthly counts, quarterly reports. You stop re-typing the same thing every week.

  1. Open Rhythms and click + New Rhythm.
  2. Pick the cadence: weekly, monthly, annual, or a custom pattern.
  3. Add the steps the rhythm should generate each cycle (e.g. “Proofread bulletin,” “Send to print,” “Set up handouts”).
  4. Save. The next set of task instances appears automatically, ready for the planner.
Why this mattersRhythms turn “I have to remember to do X every week” into “X will be on my planner every week without me thinking about it.” That's the whole point.
Adjusting or pausing a rhythm
  • Open a rhythm to add or remove steps — existing future instances regenerate to match.
  • Pause a rhythm if you go on sabbatical or the activity stops; resume it later without losing the template.
  • Deleting a rhythm removes future instances; tasks already in the planner stay where they are.
Date-driven work

Projects Everyone

Projects are for annual or one-time efforts with a target date — Christmas service, retreat weekend, an annual budget. You define the template once, with steps measured as offsets (“6 weeks before,” “2 weeks before,” “3 days after”). Every time you spin up an instance with a real target date, Rhythm calendarizes the whole thing.

  1. Open Projects and click + New Template.
  2. Add steps with their offset from the target date — e.g. “Order materials — 8 weeks before,” “Send invites — 4 weeks before,” “Debrief — 1 week after.”
  3. When the real cycle starts, click New Instance, give it a target date, and Rhythm builds out every step as a real task on the right day.
  4. Tasks from this project show up in the Weekly Planner on their offset dates, tagged to the project.
Annual projects auto-spawnTemplates marked annual generate a fresh instance each year automatically — change the year, all the offsets follow.
Editing a project mid-flight
  • Open the instance to shift its target date — every offset step recalculates to the new date.
  • Add or remove steps on an instance without touching the template (or vice versa).
  • Completed steps are frozen; rescheduling moves only what's still open.
Hands-off work

Automations Setup

Automations are rules that turn signals into work. When something happens — a Planning Center plan publishes, a Google event lands on your calendar, a rhythm instance is generated — an automation can react: create a task, link context, notify a colleague.

  1. Open Automations and click + New Rule.
  2. Pick a trigger (the signal — e.g. “PCO plan published”) and an action (what to do — “create a follow-up task,” “send a message”).
  3. Test it with Preview to make sure the right rows match.
  4. Turn it on. Rhythm runs the rule whenever the trigger fires — quietly and idempotently (no duplicates).

Recipes — six rules worth setting up on day one

These are the automations most staff end up wanting eventually. Copy the shape, swap the specifics for your context. Each card lists the trigger, the action, and why it's worth automating.

PCO plan publishes

Action: create a task "Confirm volunteer assignments — <service name>" due 5 days before the service, assigned to the worship leader.
Fires when: Planning Center marks a plan as Published.
Why: publishing is the right moment to start volunteer coordination — and easy to forget if you're staring at email.

Facility-use event lands on Google Calendar

Action: create a "Confirm setup for <event name>" task due the day before, assigned to facilities.
Fires when: a new event is added to the church Facilities Use Google Calendar.
Why: outside groups book rooms via Google; this loops their event into Rhythm so setup isn't forgotten.

Volunteer declines a PCO plan

Action: create a "Find replacement for <position> — <date>" task assigned to the worship leader, due same-day.
Fires when: a Planning Center volunteer marks themselves Declined.
Why: turns the decline notification into an actionable next step instead of an email you scroll past.

Rhythm step missed

Action: send a message to the responsible team's thread with the missed step name.
Fires when: a rhythm step's due date passes with the step still open.
Why: turns "did anyone do this?" into a one-line nudge in the right channel without finger-pointing.

Project step overdue

Action: create a "Check in on <step name>" task assigned to the project owner, due tomorrow.
Fires when: a project step's offset date passes with the step still open.
Why: annual-project slippage compounds; a 1-day check-in is the cheapest way to catch it early.

Urgent-tagged task created

Action: send a message in the relevant team thread linking the task.
Fires when: a task is created and tagged urgent.
Why: "I'll just throw it in Rhythm" only works if someone sees it. The auto-ping closes that loop.

Test before going liveUse Preview to confirm a rule matches what you expect. Rules are deterministic and idempotent, but a too-broad trigger can flood the planner with low-priority tasks.
Spaces & reservations

Facilities Everyone

Facilities is where you reserve rooms. Every staff member can see the schedule; you can book single events or recurring series. Facilities managers can book on behalf of others and edit any reservation.

  1. Open Facilities and click the + Reserve button.
  2. Pick a room, a date, a start / end time, give it a title, and (optionally) add notes (setup, equipment, contact).
  3. Save. Conflicts are caught up-front — overlaps are blocked, and for a recurring series each occurrence is reported individually.
You can edit your own

You can edit or delete reservations you created — change the time, room, or notes any time.

Recurring series

Set a weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, or custom-dates series. Series edits and deletes apply to the whole series.

Manager overview

Facilities managers see day / week / month summaries with room and building filters, and can edit anyone's reservation.

Conflict handlingIf you try to book a room that's already taken, Rhythm tells you up front. For a recurring series, conflicts on specific occurrences are flagged so the rest of the series still goes through.
What's coming later
  • Google Calendar two-way sync with the Facilities Use calendar is planned but not yet in this build.
  • Single-occurrence edits inside a recurring series aren't supported yet — for now, series edits change the whole series.
Internal chat

Messages Everyone

Messages is a lightweight in-app chat for staff — thread-based, so a conversation stays attached to its topic instead of getting scattered across email.

  1. Click + New Thread, give it a title, and pick the people who should see it.
  2. Type a message; press Enter to send. Shift + Enter adds a new line.
  3. Threads with new activity in the last 24 hours show a small dot in the list so you can find them quickly.

External signals

Integrations Everyone · setup once

Integrations is where you connect Rhythm to the systems you already use — Planning Center, Google Calendar, more to come. Once connected, signals flow in as read-only context in your week: plan items, calendar shadows, source-of-truth markers.

  1. Open Integrations.
  2. Click Connect next to the service you want — sign in with your church account when prompted.
  3. Confirm the scopes Rhythm is requesting. Rhythm only asks for what it needs (read-only by default).
  4. Once connected, the next sync window pulls in your data and it shows up in the planner.

Preferences

Settings Everyone

Don't change the Server URL unless askedPointing Rhythm at a different server is the #1 way to make your tasks look like they've disappeared — they haven't, you're just looking at a different database. Leave the API Server field on the default unless IT or finance specifically asks you to change it.

Settings holds your account and the API Server URL Rhythm talks to. For nearly everyone, both are correct out of the box and you'll never reopen this screen.

About the Server URL field
  • The default is right for 99% of staff. Rhythm ships pointed at https://api.vcrcapps.com — the church's hosted server, the only place your real tasks, rhythms, projects, and reservations live.
  • Changing it points Rhythm at a different database. If IT spins up a beta backend, or finance asks you to verify something against an isolated copy, they'll give you a different URL. Tasks you create on that other server live there only — they don't sync back to production.
  • "My tasks are gone" almost always means a changed Server URL. Open Settings; if the API Server field shows anything other than https://api.vcrcapps.com, that's why. Put it back to the default and your work reappears — it was never lost, just hidden behind the wrong address.
  • This setting is local to your Mac. Changing it here doesn't change it on your laptop, your home Mac, or anyone else's machine — every device tracks its own server preference. See Using Rhythm on more than one Mac in "More tools & tips" if you have more than one.
  • How to put it back if you changed it by accident. Open Settings, clear the API Server field, type https://api.vcrcapps.com exactly, and save. Quit and re-open Rhythm; your tasks return because you're looking at the right database again.

Handy extras you'll reach for now and then.

The Detail pane — work without losing your place
  • Click any task, project, or reservation to open its detail pane on the right. The list stays in view, so you can move through items without losing context — that's the whole point of the multi-pane layout.
Search — find anything fast
  • Every list has a search box at the top. It matches titles, notes, and (where relevant) project names. Searching is the fastest way back to a task you opened last week and forgot the title of.
Keyboard niceties
  • Cmd+N creates a new task from anywhere. Cmd+, opens Settings. Esc closes any detail pane or dialog.
Updates & the app server
  • Rhythm checks the app server on launch. If you see a "server offline" banner, it usually means you're not on the church network or VPN. Give it a moment, or contact IT.
  • When a new release lands, come back to this page and re-download — your data and preferences are kept on the server, so the new build picks them up automatically.
Notifications & reminders
  • What pings you today. Rhythm uses macOS notifications for a small set of events — most visibly, when an agent session you started finishes. Routine task due-dates are not notified; they show up in the Weekly Planner so it stays the single source of truth for "what's due."
  • Allowing macOS notifications. Open System Settings → Notifications → Rhythm and turn alerts on. If Rhythm doesn't appear in that list yet, launch the app and trigger anything that would notify (e.g. open a Claude session) — macOS registers the app the first time it tries to deliver a notification.
  • The bell on a message row. Inside an agent session, each message has a small bell toggle in its action row. Turning it on means "notify me when this session finishes," off means it stays quiet. The setting is local to your Mac and remembered between launches.
  • What Rhythm doesn't do. No email, no SMS, no push notifications to your phone. The Weekly Planner + macOS notifications + the app itself are the surfaces Rhythm uses.
  • Muting temporarily. Close the Rhythm window, sign out, or turn on macOS Do Not Disturb — any of those silences notifications without changing your data.
Using Rhythm on more than one Mac
  • Yes — install Rhythm on as many Macs as you use. Download the same .dmg from this guide on each machine.
  • Sign in with the same church account on each one. Tasks, rhythms, projects, and reservations all live on the server, so the same data shows up everywhere.
  • What's per-device, not synced: window position and size, recently-selected agent session, the Server URL setting (see Settings), and macOS notification permissions. Everything that matters for planning is server-side.
  • If you change the Server URL on one Mac, the other Macs are unaffected. That's a feature, not a bug — it means a beta-backend test on one machine doesn't pull your laptop into the wrong database.
Where your data lives
  • Every task, rhythm, project, reservation, and message is stored on the church-hosted server at https://api.vcrcapps.com — not on your Mac. Uninstalling and reinstalling Rhythm doesn't lose anything; signing in on a new Mac shows you the same data.
  • The server runs on church-managed infrastructure (see the deployment runbook) and is backed up on a regular cadence. For the current schedule (or to ask about restoring something from an older snapshot), reach out to IT.
  • If something looks missing, before assuming the worst: open Settings and check the API Server URL. A changed Server URL is the most common cause of "my data vanished" by a wide margin — putting it back to https://api.vcrcapps.com restores everything because the data was never gone, just hidden behind the wrong address.
  • If a Server URL change isn't the cause and data is genuinely missing, talk to IT before recreating anything. They can check server logs and recent backups.

Questions & fixes

The planner is empty / sparse
You probably don't have any rhythms or project instances set up yet. Add a couple of weekly rhythms for things you already do, then revisit the planner — it'll fill in.
A task I finished is back?
Finished tasks stay finished. If you see something repeat itself, it's almost certainly a rhythm generating the next instance — open the rhythm to confirm the cadence.
A project step landed on the wrong day
Open the project instance and confirm the target date — every offset step is computed from it. Adjusting the target date moves every open step automatically.
I booked a room but someone else got the slot too
That shouldn't happen — Rhythm blocks overlaps. If it did, ask a facilities manager to check Facilities → Manager Overview; they can re-assign or resolve.
My Google / Planning Center events aren't showing
Open Integrations and confirm the connection still says Connected. If it says Reconnect or shows an error, click through to re-authorize.
“Server offline” banner
You're not reaching api.vcrcapps.com — usually a network blip or you're off the church network. Wait a moment, or check with IT if it persists.
How do I update?
Come back to this page and click Download for Mac again. The new build replaces the old in Applications; your data stays put on the server.
Where do I report bugs or ideas?
Talk to AJ or finance — there's a simple internal channel for reports, and most things land as a small follow-up task in Rhythm itself.
